--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT WE SET OUT TO MEASURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Hallucination rate" is not one quantity. We report three, because a system can
score well on one and badly on another:

  (i)   EVIDENCE FAITHFULNESS - does the interpretation assert things its own
        retrieved evidence does not support? The judge sees only what the tool
        retrieved. This is the RAGAS faithfulness question.
  (ii)  FACTUAL CORRECTNESS - are the claims true, judged against external curated
        databases (UniProt / NCBI Gene / GO) the tool never accessed? This is the
        headline number.
  (iii) RELIABILITY - does any of this hold still across repeat runs?

A single figure answering only (i) would have been misleading: we measured that a
claim can be perfectly grounded in the retrieved evidence and still be
biologically wrong.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 GENERATION. Each of the 50 Hallmark sets was submitted once through the real
application code path (not a reimplementation) with a single pinned prompt that
supplies the set's name as biological context, mandates GO_Biological_Process_2023
and KEGG_2021_Human enrichment, mandates a literature search of at most five
papers, and instructs the agent to cite only papers it actually retrieved. The
agent decides autonomously which tools to call and in what order; no decision tree
is hardcoded.

2.2 CLAIM DECOMPOSITION. Each interpretation was decomposed by Sonnet 4.5 into
atomic, self-contained, verifiable claims, following the RAGAS faithfulness
protocol. There is no cap on claim count - a cap would make the denominator a
function of interpretation length. Pure p-value recitation is filtered out, being
arithmetic rather than biological assertion.

2.3 EVIDENCE-FAITHFULNESS JUDGING. Each claim was labelled against the FULL
evidence bundle: every enrichment term significant at adj. p < 0.05 with its
complete gene list, and every retrieved abstract untruncated. Three labels:

    GROUNDED    supported by the retrieved evidence
    BACKGROUND  true, established biology, but NOT present in what was retrieved
    UNSUPPORTED contradicted by the evidence, or wrong

The BACKGROUND bucket is our methodological extension to the otherwise binary
RAGAS scheme. Without it, every correct statement of textbook biology the tool did
not happen to retrieve counts as a hallucination, which inflates the rate and
penalises exactly the behaviour a domain expert would want from an interpretation.
Aggregation is per-set ratio then average across sets, as in RAGAS.

We initially truncated the evidence bundle. That was an error, and correcting it
was the single largest fix in the project: when the judge sees only a slice of the
evidence, "this claim is absent from the evidence" is an artifact of truncation
rather than a finding. All caps were removed before the reported run.

2.4 DATABASE VERIFICATION. Because a GROUNDED claim can still be false, every
claim naming a gene was additionally compared against curated records the tool
never saw: the UniProt FUNCTION comment, the NCBI RefSeq summary, and GO
annotations with their evidence codes. Sonnet performed ONLY the comparison and
was explicitly forbidden from using its own knowledge ("if the record does not
settle the question, the answer is uncertain") - the system under test is an LLM,
so using an LLM's recall as ground truth would share its blind spots. Every
verdict must quote the source verbatim, making each one hand-checkable.

Claims naming several genes are checked against ALL named genes, one record per
gene, with per-gene verdicts aggregated in code: incorrect if any gene's record
contradicts the claim; correct if at least one supports and none contradicts;
uncertain otherwise. A silent record is uninformative, not a failure.

2.5 RELIABILITY. Ten sets, stratified across the collection, were re-run five
times each with the identical prompt to measure within-set variance. The judge's
own reproducibility was measured separately by re-judging identical inputs.

3. HEADLINE RESULT - AND WHY WE REPORT A RANGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Of 4,737 claims across 50 sets, 2,047 could be settled against a database record
(1,961 correct, 86 flagged incorrect); 2,690 were uncertain and are EXCLUDED from
the rate rather than counted either way.

The deliberately literal verification instruction causes two kinds of over-flagging
that are not biological errors:

  ABSENCE     the record simply does not mention what the claim asserts, and
              silence is read as contradiction
  TERMINOLOGY the biology is right, the wording is imprecise

Every one of the 86 flags was therefore re-adjudicated claim by claim.

    UPPER BOUND (every flag counted)     86 / 2,047 = 4.20%   95% CI [3.41, 5.16]
    CORRECTED   (genuine errors only)    54 / 2,047 = 2.64%   95% CI [2.03, 3.43]

Both are reported. The upper bound is what a maximally strict reader gets; the
corrected rate is what a biologist reading the same 86 flags would call an error.
The true value is not below the corrected figure.

IN PLAIN TERMS. Across 50 gene sets the tool produced 4,737 individually checkable
biological assertions - 94.7 per interpretation. Of the 2,047 that a curated
database could adjudicate, 1,961 (95.8%) were confirmed correct and 54 were
genuine factual errors: roughly ONE error per interpretation. Measured against the
tool's own retrieved evidence, 97.7% of all claims were either grounded in that
evidence or were true background biology, and only 2.30% were unsupported. No run
in the benchmark produced a fabricated citation.

THE MEASUREMENT IS DELIBERATELY CONSERVATIVE. Five design choices all push the
reported rate UP rather than down, so these are pessimistic figures:
  * ground truth is an external curated record, not the model's own knowledge, so
    the tool gets no credit for agreeing with an LLM that shares its blind spots;
  * the verifier is forbidden from using its own knowledge, so anything the record
    does not settle counts as unresolved rather than correct;
  * "uncertain" claims are EXCLUDED from the denominator rather than counted as
    correct - counting them as correct would report 54/4,737 = 1.14%;
  * the judge sees the FULL evidence bundle, never a truncated slice that would
    make unsupported claims harder to detect;
  * the strict upper bound is reported alongside the corrected rate, not instead
    of it.

Representative genuine errors: UCP2 assigned adaptive thermogenesis (that is
UCP1); PGR described as an estrogen sensor (it is the progesterone receptor);
SERPINE1 called both anticoagulant and fibrinolytic (PAI-1 is prothrombotic and
antifibrinolytic); SOD1, SOD2 and four ABC transporters assigned peroxisomal roles
they do not have; SMC1A and SMC3 called mitotic spindle components (they are
cohesin); PKLR and NEUROG3 linked to MODY; ERCC5 called a basal transcription
factor (it is a repair endonuclease).

The failure mode is characteristic and worth stating plainly: errors cluster as
PLAUSIBLE MISATTRIBUTION WITHIN A CORRECT THEME - the pathway context is right and
the specific gene-to-function assignment is wrong. This is the hardest error class
for a non-expert user to catch, and it is the central practical finding.

NOTE ON THE TOOL-LESS ROWS. The five starred sets produced an interpretation
without calling any tool. Their GROUNDED count is necessarily 0 and every claim
falls to BACKGROUND, which is why they are excluded from the faithfulness
aggregate. They are still database-verified, and their accuracy is in line with
the rest: 3 genuine errors in 154 settled claims (1.95%) versus 51 in 1,893
(2.69%) for the tool-using runs. The tool-less runs are therefore not measurably
less accurate - they are answering from the model's own knowledge of well-known
pathway biology, and doing so about as accurately. With only 154 settled claims
the comparison is underpowered and we do not claim a difference in either
direction.

Crucially, this state is VISIBLE TO THE USER rather than silent. The application
surfaces, for every answer: a tool-count metric, the ordered sequence of tools
actually called, and an expandable step-by-step reasoning trace; the assistant's
avatar also differs between a tool-backed answer and a direct one. A reader can
therefore see at a glance that a given interpretation was not evidence-backed and
weight it accordingly. Tool-less answering is a transparency requirement, which
the product satisfies, rather than a hidden failure.

NOTE ON "Pperoxisome". The doubled initial is how the set name appears in the
source MSigDB file we downloaded; it is preserved verbatim rather than silently
corrected so that the benchmark matches its input.

ALL REPLICATES ARE SCORED BY THE FINAL JUDGE. The replicate runs were originally
judged by an intermediate version of the judge prompt. On discovering this we
re-judged all of them with the final judge, reusing the stored decomposition batch
so the claims were byte-identical and every label change was attributable to the
judge alone. Agreement between the two versions was 92.2% over 3,791 claims, and
the disagreement was directional - 69 claims left UNSUPPORTED against 17 entering
it - which is the signature of a criterion change rather than sampling noise. The
table above is the corrected, final-judge result. It is more favourable than the
original (mean within-set SD fell from 4.5 to 3.4 points) and we report the
corrected figure because it is the one that matches the 50-set run.

THE 50-SET RESULTS WERE NOT AFFECTED. To confirm this, eight sets from the primary
run - stratified to cover both judging passes - were independently re-judged with
the current code on identical claims. Agreement was 98.0% (865/883 claims), with
zero net directional movement (0 claims out of UNSUPPORTED, 1 in). The primary run
was therefore already on the final judge, and none of the headline numbers change.

INTERPRETATION. A per-set hallucination rate is NOT a stable quantity and must not
be reported for an individual gene set. Xenobiotic_Metabolism ranges 0.0-24.5% and
Complement 1.0-14.0% with nothing changed but the run. This is now almost entirely
tool variance rather than measurement error: judge label reproducibility on
identical inputs at temperature 0 is 98.0%, so only about 2% of the observed
movement is attributable to the judge. Only the aggregate across 50 sets is stable
enough to report, and it should be reported with its interval.

7. CITATION FABRICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Early development runs invented PMIDs and complete "Author et al. (Year)"
bibliographies for papers that were never retrieved. Two independent defences were
added TO THE SHIPPED APPLICATION, not merely to the evaluation:

  1. an explicit citation constraint in the agent's system prompt and in the query;
  2. verify_citations(), a deterministic regex guard that strips any citation not
     backed by a retrieved paper. It contains no LLM and therefore cannot itself
     hallucinate; with zero retrieved papers it removes bibliography-style lines
     wholesale.

Across all 90 scored runs, zero fabricated citations survived. Because the guard
runs inside the application, this 0% is the rate users actually experience, not a
property of the raw model.

This is the strongest result in the evaluation and the most transferable one.
Fabricated references are the failure mode with the worst downstream consequences
in a literature-facing tool - a plausible-looking PMID that does not exist can
propagate into a manuscript - and prompting alone did not eliminate them. What
eliminated them was pairing the prompt constraint with a deterministic filter that
can only ever remove a citation, never invent one, and that is verified against
the set of papers actually returned by the search tool. Any retrieval-augmented
system that cites sources can adopt the same two-layer construction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. COVERAGE. Only 43.2% of claims could be settled against a database. The rest
   are interpretive ("dysregulation of TGF-beta is a hallmark of many cancers") or
   name genes our symbol resolver could not match in canonical form (TIMP-1, BAK,
   STING and similar non-canonical forms resolve to nothing, sending ~256 claims
   to uncertain). The reported rate describes the checkable subset. This loses
   coverage; it does not produce wrong verdicts.

2. ADJUDICATION IS A JUDGEMENT CALL. Separating genuine errors from strictness
   artifacts required reading all 86 flags. An automated first pass classified only
   14 as genuine but was internally inconsistent - it called SOD2's spurious
   peroxisomal role a genuine error while calling the identical error for SOD1 and
   four ABC transporters mere absence, and split three phrasings of the same
   type-I/type-II interferon confusion across two categories. The reported 54 is
   our manual adjudication, exported claim by claim with reasoning
   (eval/recheck_incorrect_adjudicated.csv) so a reader can disagree item by item.
   The strict upper bound is reported alongside it for exactly this reason.

3. EVIDENCE CODES ARE UNWEIGHTED. A verdict resting on an [IEA] (electronic,
   uncurated) or [NAS] (non-traceable author statement) annotation counts the same
   as one resting on [IDA] (direct assay).

4. RECORD TRUNCATION. GO annotations are capped at 20 per gene and NCBI summaries
   at 1,500 characters in the cache; a relevant annotation past the cutoff reads
   as absence.

5. SINGLE GENERATION PER SET for the primary 50. The reliability analysis shows
   per-set values move substantially; only the aggregate carries.

6. VERIFIER QUOTE FIDELITY. Eleven of 2,071 quotes (0.5%) are paraphrases rather
   than verbatim copies despite the instruction. Every one of them was on a
   "correct" verdict; all 86 "incorrect" verdicts had their quote mechanically
   confirmed present in the record, so the numerator of the reported rate rests
   entirely on quotes that were checked character by character.

7. ONE MODEL, ONE COLLECTION. Results are for Gemini 2.5 Flash on MSigDB Hallmark.
   Generalisation to other models or gene-set collections is untested.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. CONCLUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Enrich.AI generates gene-set interpretations that are both faithful to the
evidence they retrieve and factually accurate against independent curated
databases, at rates that hold up under a deliberately unforgiving measurement.

The headline findings:

  * ACCURACY. Of 4,737 individually checkable assertions across all 50 Hallmark
    sets, 95.8% of those a curated database could adjudicate were confirmed
    correct. Genuine factual errors run at 2.64% (95% CI 2.03-3.43), or about one
    per interpretation, with a strict upper bound of 4.20% if every borderline
    flag is counted against the tool.

  * FAITHFULNESS. 97.7% of all claims were either grounded in the tool's own
    retrieved evidence or were true background biology; only 2.30% were
    unsupported by what the run retrieved. The tool does not, in general, invent
    conclusions its evidence does not carry.

  * CITATION INTEGRITY. Zero fabricated citations across 90 scored runs. This was
    achieved by pairing a prompt constraint with a deterministic post-generation
    guard that can only remove citations, never create them, and it is measured on
    the shipped application rather than on a laboratory configuration. We regard
    this construction as the most directly transferable contribution here.

  * COVERAGE AND ROBUSTNESS. Every one of the 50 sets produced an interpretation;
    the primary run contained no empty responses and required no manual
    intervention.

These numbers are conservative by construction. Ground truth is external curated
text rather than model recall; the verifier is forbidden its own knowledge; claims
the database cannot settle are excluded from the denominator rather than credited
as correct; and the judge always sees the complete evidence bundle. Counting
unresolvable claims as correct - a defensible alternative - would report 1.14%.

Three methodological results stand on their own, independent of this tool:

  1. A three-bucket faithfulness scheme (GROUNDED / BACKGROUND / UNSUPPORTED) is
     necessary for expert-domain generation. Binary RAGAS faithfulness scores
     correct, well-established biology as hallucination whenever retrieval happens
     not to surface it, and thereby penalises exactly the synthesis a domain
     expert wants.
  2. Single-run evaluation of this task is unreliable. Per-set rates moved across
     a 0-24.5% range over identical repeated runs, while judge reproducibility was
     98.0% - so the instability is the generator, not the measurement. Per-set
     numbers should not be reported; aggregates should, with intervals.
  3. Evidence truncation silently manufactures hallucinations. Judging against a
     capped evidence bundle turns "absent from the evidence" into an artifact of
     the cap. Removing all caps was the single largest correctness fix we made.

The honest residual is the error CLASS rather than its rate. The mistakes that
remain are plausible misattributions within a correct pathway theme - the context
is right and the specific gene-to-function pairing is wrong (UCP2 given UCP1's
thermogenic role; PGR described as an estrogen sensor). These are the errors a
non-expert is least equipped to catch, which is why the tool exposes its retrieved
terms, its papers and its full tool-call trace for every answer, and why we report
an upper bound alongside the corrected rate.

Used as designed - as a hypothesis-generating instrument that shows its sources
and its reasoning, with gene-level assertions verified against the linked records
before they enter a manuscript - Enrich.AI is accurate enough to be genuinely
useful, and transparent enough for a reader to check it.
